## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Work only within the user's stated scope. Do not pre-write later units, batches, settings, dialogue, or scenes.
- Keep the workflow document-centered. Do not build an API client, server, web UI, or external generation application.
- Treat the initial maximum as concept through the first final candidate. Require explicit approval for unit 2 onward.
- Use batches of at most three narrative units after the first unit is approved, then stop for review.
- When asked only to inspect, review, audit, plan, or propose, do not create or modify files unless explicitly requested.

## Approval and scope gates

- Use one explicit approval-state document as the source of truth.
- Check it before an outline, draft, final, revision, batch, or canon change.
- Halt if approval is missing, stale, unclear, or inconsistent with the request.
- Compare final registry, unit status, and final files before final-related work.
- Treat a request for more than three continuation units as authorization for the first safe three only.
- End every completed batch in a user-review state.

## Originality and canon

- Use genre conventions, not another work's unique premise, terminology, signature scene, characters, or an identifiable author's style.
- Translate references to existing works into abstract reader expectations or narrative functions.
- Record material similarity concerns and halt finalization until differentiated.
- Do not change established canon directly. Propose the exact change, conflict, reason, tradeoffs, and affected files; wait for approval.
- Keep story, character, rules, canon, timeline, foreshadowing, payoff, and rolling-context records synchronized.

## Narrative quality

Require each unit to contain:

1. an early hook;
2. a meaningful protagonist choice;
3. a concrete reader reward;
4. an ending that drives the next unit.

Review setting continuity, rule compliance, character voice and motivation, chronology, style, originality, content/rights risks, cast load, unresolved threads, repeated patterns, and reward strength. Log ability or special-rule usage at outline, draft, review, and final stages, including permission level, cost/limit, and violations.

## Version and state integrity

- Never edit an existing final in place. Create the next version and a matching revision note.
- Distinguish approved finals from pending final candidates.
- Update the final registry whenever a candidate or approved final changes.
- Keep unit status and registry consistent; mismatch is a critical issue.
- Synchronize new, delayed, and resolved foreshadowing in both foreshadowing and payoff records.
- Accumulate quality, reader reward, pattern, cast, and run-report indexes when those files exist.

## Feedback and recovery

Classify feedback as one or more of `TEXT_ONLY`, `STYLE_ADJUSTMENT`, `CHARACTER_VOICE`, `SCENE_REWRITE`, `CONTINUITY_REPAIR`, `BIBLE_CHANGE_REQUIRED`, or `SCOPE_EXPANSION_RISK`.

Keep style or voice feedback at sentence/dialogue scope unless the user authorizes broader changes. If immediate application was not requested, record an impact matrix and application plan first. Allow one automatic revision pass by default.

For a critical issue, stop finalization and record:

- `halt_reason_code`;
- concise problem and impact;
- related files;
- safe reversible remedy;
- user decision required;
- minimum safe resume scope.

## Completion gate

Do not call narrative, review, revision, batch, or packaging work complete without its required review, revision note, status, and run report. Update a run-report index when present. Report changed files, checks, conflicts, quality concerns, pending decisions, and safe next-task candidates without generating future story content.
